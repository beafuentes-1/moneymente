## APP | How MoneyMente works
MoneyMente is a personal financial advisor. Enter your income, expenses by category, debts, savings and goals in My numbers. The Overview shows your health score, your next best move and your action plan. Goals plans each target, Year plan forecasts the next 12 months with special expenses, and Report makes a printable summary. Ask Buck anything in the chat, use the voice menu for audio and the language menu for Spanish or English.
## L1 | Know your numbers
Step 1 is knowing where your money goes. A simple guide is 50% needs, 30% wants and 20% saving and paying debt. Your surplus is income minus expenses, and the savings rate is the surplus divided by income.
## L2 | Starter emergency fund
Step 2 is a starter emergency fund of $1,000, or one month of expenses if that is lower. It protects you from small surprises so you do not borrow again. Do this before attacking debt.
## L3 | Defeat your debt
Step 3 is paying off debt. Snowball pays the smallest balance first for quick motivation. Avalanche pays the highest interest rate first and usually costs less in interest. Pay the minimum on every debt and put all extra money on the target debt.
## L4 | Full emergency fund
Step 4 grows your emergency fund to three months of expenses. This is your safety net if you lose your income. The runway number shows how many months of expenses your savings cover.
## L5 | Savings goals
Step 5 is making sure every goal has a plan with a target amount, a date and a monthly amount. A goal is on track when the base scenario projection reaches the target by the date. Raise the monthly amount or move the date if a goal is behind.
## L6 | Your first investment
Step 6 is making your first investment once you have a safety net and no costly debt. Beginners often use low-cost diversified index funds, invest a fixed amount every month and stay invested for years. Prices go up and down, and you can lose money, so only invest what you will not need soon.
## L7 | Retirement and compound interest
Step 7 shows how time grows money. Move the sliders for years, yearly return and monthly contribution to see the portfolio value versus what you contributed. The returns shown are examples, not promises.
## G1 | Compound interest
Compound interest means you earn returns on your earlier returns too. The longer your money stays invested, the faster it grows. Starting early matters more than starting big.
## G2 | Index fund
An index fund holds many companies at once to follow a market index. It is usually low cost and diversified. It is a common starting point for beginners, but it still carries risk.
## G3 | Diversification and risk
Diversification means spreading money across many investments so one bad result hurts less. Risk is the chance that returns are lower than expected or negative. Higher possible returns usually come with higher risk.
## G4 | Inflation
Inflation is the general rise in prices over time, so the same money buys less later. Savings that earn less than inflation lose buying power. This is one reason people invest for the long term.
## G5 | Interest rate and APR
The interest rate is the yearly cost of a debt or the yearly return on savings. High-rate debt, like many credit cards, grows fast, so paying it down is often a strong move. Compare debts by their rate per year.
## G6 | Emergency fund runway
Runway is your savings divided by your monthly expenses. It tells you how many months you could pay your bills with no income. Three months is the goal in level 4.
## B1 | Monthly budget by category
The budget card lists your monthly spending by category, such as housing, food, transport, utilities, health, entertainment and shopping. Mark each as a need or a want. The total feeds your expenses, and the charts show where your money goes against the 50/30/20 guide. To stop overspending, start with your biggest category and with wants like subscriptions and eating out.
## G7 | Pay yourself first
Paying yourself first means moving money to savings or investments as soon as you are paid, before spending on anything else. Automating the transfer makes saving a habit. Even a small fixed amount each month builds up.
## S1 | Financial health score
The health score from 0 to 100 combines five parts: saving rate, safety net (months of expenses saved), debt compared with yearly income, spending balance versus the 50/30/20 guide, and investing. Each part shows its own score so you can see what to improve first. It is an educational estimate, not a credit score.
## GP1 | Goal planner and scenarios
The goal planner shows how much you need to save each month to reach a target by its date. Scenarios apply a conservative 3%, base 6% and optimistic 9% yearly return to the money set aside. Goals are on track, close or behind based on the base scenario. Short goals are usually safer in cash than in investments.
## Y1 | Year plan
The year plan forecasts the next 12 months of income, expenses and balance. Add special expenses like holidays, tuition or taxes, and special income like bonuses, in the month they happen. The chart shows months with a deficit and your lowest balance. You can also enter your real last 12 months to compare.
## R1 | Printable report
The Report tab builds a summary with your health score, budget, debts, goals, forecast and action plan. Press Print or Save as PDF and choose Save as PDF in your browser.
